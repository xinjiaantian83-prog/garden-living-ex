## Observed Tendencies

### 1. Distance From Building

The model strongly preferred the foreground open ground rather than the base of the building.

Most results kept the water stand several meters away from the wall. It did not naturally attach the product to the facade, near a drain line, or near an existing utility point.

Interpretation:

The model appears to treat the water stand as a visible garden object to be showcased, not as a plumbing fixture that should be placed against a wall or utility route.

### 2. Left / Right Position

The model distributed the product across the foreground, but right and center placements were slightly more common.

- Left foreground: 5/20
- Center foreground: 7/20
- Right foreground: 7/20
- Right staged placement: 1/20

Interpretation:

Without a placement rule, the model does not infer a strong utility-based location. It chooses visually open areas where the product is easy to see.

### 3. Product Direction

The water stand usually faces the camera or slightly toward the center of the image.

The faucet side is generally visible. The model rarely turns the product away from the viewer.

Interpretation:

The model prioritizes product visibility over construction logic.

### 4. Garden Pan Direction

The garden pan usually opens toward the viewer with the inside visible.

This produces good product recognition, but it is not always the most natural construction orientation.

Interpretation:

The reference images taught the model to preserve the pan shape well, but also biased the pan toward a product-display angle.

### 5. Walking Path / Movement

Many placements are in the middle of the open yard and would likely interfere with movement or future garden use.

Edge placements are less frequent. Runs 10, 14, and 18 are closer to an edge and feel less obstructive than the center placements.

Interpretation:

The model does not understand garden circulation or practical utility placement unless explicitly told.

### 6. Extra Scene Changes

Several outputs added or changed surrounding landscaping, paving, or planting around the product.

This was especially visible in runs 06 and 07.

Interpretation:

Even with product-only intent, the model may beautify the surrounding area to make the object feel installed. This is a useful visual instinct, but it can drift away from faithful before/after simulation.

## AI Preferred Placement Summary

When no placement rule is given, the model tends to:

1. Place the water stand in the foreground.
2. Keep it clearly visible and camera-facing.
3. Avoid placing it directly against the building.
4. Prefer open soil over edge or utility-based placement.
5. Add a small staged base, planting, paving, or cleanup around it.
6. Prioritize product presentation over real plumbing logic.

## Conclusion

The model has good product visibility instincts, but weak practical placement instincts.

For Version 0.4, the important finding is:

> Without placement rules, the model does not naturally choose the most construction-logical position for a water stand. It chooses the most visible foreground position.

This is useful for understanding the next development step, but no placement improvement has been implemented in this version.

## Confirmed Common Tendencies

These tendencies are now treated as confirmed Version 0.4 observations:

- When no placement is specified, the image generation model prioritizes placing the product in the foreground where it is easy to see, rather than in a construction-logical position.
- The product tends to face the camera.
- The model does not sufficiently consider building distance, circulation, plumbing, utility routes, or other construction conditions on its own.
- Empty open space is treated as a product display area.
- The model may add unrequested surrounding staging such as planting, paving, grading, or cleanup to make the product feel installed.

Water stand-specific improvement is closed at this point. Further water stand placement rules are intentionally not added in Version 0.4.
