## Observed Tendencies

### 1. "囲う" Is Interpreted As A Front Fence Line

The model did not create an L-shape, U-shape, or full perimeter enclosure.

All 10 results placed the American fence across the front side of the garden, closest to the viewer.

Interpretation:

Without layout rules, the model treats "enclose the garden" as "show a visible fence line in the foreground."

### 2. The Fence Becomes A Product Display Surface

The fence is usually placed camera-facing and large in the foreground.

This makes the product easy to see, but it does not necessarily define a usable dog run or enclosed garden.

Interpretation:

The model still prioritizes product visibility over spatial logic.

### 3. Gates Are Sometimes Invented

Runs 02, 03, and 09 created a center gate-like opening or hinged panel.

This was not explicitly requested.

Interpretation:

When asked to "enclose" an area, the model sometimes adds an entrance because it assumes a fenced area needs a gate. This can be useful, but it is not controlled.

### 4. Garden Usability Is Only Partially Represented

The fence line does not reliably wrap around the usable garden area.

It often behaves like a visual boundary between camera and yard, rather than a real enclosure.

Interpretation:

The model needs future placement guidance if the goal is to show "a dog can safely run inside this area" or "the family can use this enclosed yard."

### 5. Height And Panel Spacing Are Mostly Natural

Fence height, rounded panel frames, chain-link mesh, and repeated support posts are generally plausible.

The model tends to keep a low garden-fence scale rather than creating a tall privacy fence.

Interpretation:

Product scale is acceptable. The weakness is not product height, but route selection.

### 6. Unrequested Surrounding Changes Can Occur

Run 09 changed the yard into artificial turf.

Other runs cleaned or smoothed soil more subtly.

Interpretation:

The model may infer a completed dog-run or garden scene and add ground improvements even when only the fence was selected.

## AI Interpretation Summary

When no specific placement is given, the model tends to interpret "庭をアメリカンフェンスで囲う" as:

1. Add a visible straight fence line in the foreground.
2. Face the fence toward the camera.
3. Sometimes add a center gate.
4. Avoid wrapping the fence around the whole garden.
5. Preserve product scale reasonably well.
6. Occasionally improve the ground surface or add unintended landscaping.

## Conclusion

The American fence behaves differently from a single object such as a water stand.

The model understands the product visually, but it does not understand the enclosure route.

The most important Version 0.4 finding is:

> For long linear products, the model defaults to a camera-facing straight display line unless the desired route is specified.

This analysis confirms that future placement work for American fence should focus on route control, not product reproduction.
